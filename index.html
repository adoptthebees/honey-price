    Where the numbers come from

    The values of the countries and the World come from FAOSTAT, the statistics database of the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). FAOSTAT does not count honey itself. It collects values from national authorities. The quotes below are FAO's own descriptions. The section "United States" comes from the Honey reports of USDA NASS; its source line gives the report.

    Production

    “The main data source is official statistics from FAO member countries. The source data can originate from surveys, administrative data and estimates based on expert observations. … If no data from either official or unofficial sources is available, data are imputed. In all cases, data are flagged accordingly.”
    “Aggregates are the sum of available data. Aggregates include estimated data.”

    Producer price

    “Agriculture producer prices are prices received by farmers for primary crops, live animals and livestock primary products as collected at the point of initial sale (prices paid at the farm gate).”
    “Most countries report annual prices from surveys, in some cases administered prices are provided. … No information is requested, and therefore available, on sample sizes at country level.”

    Export price

    “The data is mainly provided by UNSD, Eurostat, and other national authorities as needed. The source data is checked for outliers, trade partner data is used for non-reporting countries or missing cells, and data on food aid is added to take into account total cross-border trade flows.”

    This page divides export value by export quantity. The result is a customs average. It mixes grades, pack sizes and re-exports. It is not a price quote or a retail price.

    Values are revised after publication

    “The questionnaire is sent annually to countries and collects national data referring to the past three years.”

    FAO data collection: crop and livestock production questionnaire

    So FAO collects the latest three production years again each year, and national offices revise their own values. The page marks the last three years of all values as provisional. Example (checked 2026-10-10):

    • US producer price, 2024: USDA reported $2.69 per pound on 14 March 2025 and $2.41 on 13 March 2026, because more 2024 honey sold in 2025. The FAOSTAT price file of 2026-10-09 still had $2.69.
    • US production, 2024: FAOSTAT has 60,960 tonnes (file of 2025-12-31). USDA has 134,393 thousand pounds, about 60,960 tonnes. They agree.

    How the ranges and scores are made

    FAOSTAT publishes no error for its values. The ranges and scores here are this tool's estimates. They are not FAO values and not statistical confidence intervals.

    Range. No value is exact, so every value has a range. The range has four parts:

      Tests.

        Totals. A world total takes its range and score from the countries that add up to it. FAO flags the sum official or estimated whatever its parts are, so the flag says little. The range assumes that country errors are independent. If they are not, the range is too narrow.

        Score. The score runs from 5 to 90. It starts from the source of the value and loses points for each problem that the tool can measure. It is a rubric, not a probability. The weights are a judgment: no test can measure them. Hover over a score to see its deductions.

          Limits. A low score does not make a value wrong, and a high score does not make it exact. The score cannot detect an error that a country repeats every year. In the tests, revisions of the latest year went up, so the range can sit too low for that year.

            What this page cannot tell you

            • It has no retail or wholesale prices. FAOSTAT has none.
            • All prices are in current US dollars, not adjusted for inflation. A 1970 price and a 2024 price do not compare directly.
            • Producer prices do not compare across countries. Methods differ, and some countries report administered prices.
            • These values can differ from the latest values of a national statistics office. See the example above.
