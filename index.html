    Where these numbers come from

    All figures come from FAOSTAT, the statistics database of the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO). FAOSTAT does not count honey. It collects figures from national authorities, so it is one step away from the original source. The quotes below are FAO's own descriptions of each data set.

    Production

    “The main data source is official statistics from FAO member countries. The source data can originate from surveys, administrative data and estimates based on expert observations. Which type of source is used by countries can significantly affect reliability and comparability of data. … In instances where no official data is available, data from unofficial sources (as specialized international commodity institutes i.e. Oil World, USDA etc.) may be used. If no data from either official or unofficial sources is available, data are imputed. In all cases, data are flagged accordingly.”
    “Aggregates are the sum of available data. Aggregates include estimated data.”

    Producer price

    “Agriculture producer prices are prices received by farmers for primary crops, live animals and livestock primary products as collected at the point of initial sale (prices paid at the farm gate).”
    “The main source is official statistics from FAO member countries. Most countries report annual prices from surveys, in some cases administered prices are provided. … No information is requested, and therefore available, on sample sizes at country level.”
    “Mostly through annual questionnaires complemented by internet data (Eurostat, some developed countries) and CountrySTAT data. Imputation of missing Price data.”

    Export price

    “The data is mainly provided by UNSD, Eurostat, and other national authorities as needed. The source data is checked for outliers, trade partner data is used for non-reporting countries or missing cells, and data on food aid is added to take into account total cross-border trade flows.”
    “UN Comtrade is the United Nations’ official global repository of international merchandise trade statistics, maintained by UNSD”
    “For this particular data domain, data sourced from UNSD, Eurostat, and WTO and only marginally processed by FAO might be subject to additional restrictions. Please consult the copyright notice of these organizations for more details.”

    This page divides the export value by the export quantity. The result is a customs average. It mixes grades, pack sizes and re-exports. It is not a price quote and not a retail price.

    A country has an export price here only if it exported at least 100 tonnes in that year. An average over a few tonnes is not reliable: from 2010 to 2024, 17% to 39% of the country years below 100 tonnes had prices outside $0.40 to $6 per lb, against 2% from 100 tonnes up. In 2024 the countries above the limit made 99.9% of all exported tonnage (checked on 2026-10-10). The world export price uses FAO's World row, which counts every exporter.

    Figures change after publication

    “The questionnaire is sent annually to countries and collects national data referring to the past three years.”

    FAO data collection, crop and livestock production questionnaire

    So FAO collects the latest three years again each year, and national offices revise their own figures. Example, checked on 2026-10-10:

    • United States, 2024 price. The USDA report of 14 March 2025 gave $2.69 per pound. The report of 13 March 2026 gives $2.41, because more 2024 honey sold in 2025. The FAOSTAT price file dated 2026-10-09 still had $2.69.
    • United States, 2024 production. FAOSTAT has 60,960 tonnes. USDA has 134,393 thousand pounds, which is about 60,960 tonnes. They agree.

    What the marks mean

    What this page cannot tell you

    • It has no retail or wholesale prices. FAOSTAT has none.
    • All prices are in current US dollars. They are not adjusted for inflation, so a price in 1970 and a price in 2024 are not directly comparable.
    • Producer prices are not comparable between countries. Methods differ, and some countries report administered prices.
    • These figures can differ from the latest figures of a national statistics office. See the example above.
